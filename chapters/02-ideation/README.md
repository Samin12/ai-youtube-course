# Chapter 2: Ideation

**Stage 1 of 8:** **Ideation** → Research → Packaging → Script → Film → Edit → Repurpose → Publish

## Why this matters

"Everything else is downstream" of the topic. *(Marcus Jones)* Most videos are won or lost here, before you ever hit record. We know our viewer and our offer. Now: what do they actually want to watch?

## What you'll learn

- **Revealed preference.** What people click beats what they say they want. Everyone says they want to eat healthy, then they order takeout. So start with what your viewer already chooses to watch. Ask: "What evidence says the people I want as customers are already choosing to watch this?" *(Jake Trinder)*
- **Outliers.** A video that massively beat its channel's average, like 100K views on a channel that averages 10K. Outliers are proof people want that idea. Look in your niche and in other niches. *(Aprilynne Alter)*
- **Floor and ceiling.** Search your customer's problem on YouTube. The floor is what a decent video on it gets. The ceiling is what the best one gets. Check the views are real and the demand is still current. Then ask: how can I raise the ceiling? *(Jake Trinder)*
- **Stack demand.** Tie your topic to something bigger people already want. "Alex Hormozi's lead generation strategy," made for Instantly, got 473,000 views and still gets 500+ views a day from search. *(Jake Trinder)*
- **Borrow formats, but know why they work.** "The new rules of…" won in SEO (search engine optimization), then LinkedIn, then for Gary Vee. *(Jake Trinder)* Take a topic from your niche and a format from anywhere: "Coding was hard until I learned these five things" becomes "Video editing was hard until…" *(Marcus Jones)*
- **Overfitting.** Copying a format without knowing why it worked. It looks the same but doesn't perform. *(Jake Trinder)*
- **Ideas that serve your offer.** "Your highest viewed video can be one of your least valuable" if it brings the wrong viewers. Instantly wanted a feature video; Jake made "Watch me sell a service in 10 hours" with the product built in. *(Jake Trinder)*

## What you'll build

[`my-video/01-idea.md`](../../my-video/01-idea.md): one idea, with proof people want it and how it leads to your offer.

## Steps

1. **Open Claude in this repo,** so it can see your `CLAUDE.md`, viewer avatar and offer.
2. **Get seed keywords.** Run [`prompts/seed-keywords.md`](prompts/seed-keywords.md). Search each keyword on YouTube and follow the outliers. *(Jake Trinder's method)*
3. **Find 5–10 outliers** for your topic, in your niche and nearby niches. Note each one's views and the channel's average. Check the floor and ceiling.
4. **Ask what problems you can solve.** Run [`prompts/audience-problems.md`](prompts/audience-problems.md). *(Nate Curtiss)*
5. **Generate ideas.** Run [`prompts/idea-generator.md`](prompts/idea-generator.md) with your outliers pasted in.
6. **Pick one.** Run your favorites through the checklist below. Save the winner in `my-video/01-idea.md`.

**How I do it:** I save outliers to a Poppy board (a visual board where you save videos and chat with AI about them), then ask AI to ideate with my avatar, offer and outliers as context. *[Coming soon: my full walkthrough]*

## Check it

- [ ] Is there proof people already watch it (outliers, a high ceiling)?
- [ ] Would your viewer avatar click it?
- [ ] Does it lead back to your offer?
- [ ] Idea filter: Can you make it? Would a stranger care? Would 80% of your audience care? Can you package it? Are you at least 7/10 excited? *(Aprilynne Alter)*
- [ ] Is it yours, not a copy? "A clone will never perform as well as the original." *(Aprilynne Alter)*

## Tips

- **Make a "burner" YouTube account** and train it by watching your competitors, so its homepage shows you outliers. *(Nate Curtiss)*
- **Tools:** the 1of10 browser extension shows outlier scores *(Nate Curtiss)*; Poppy for saving and searching them.
- **Keep a running list** of ideas you're excited about. There's a spot for it in `my-video/01-idea.md`.
