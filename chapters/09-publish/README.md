# Chapter 9: Publish

**Stage 8 of 8:** Ideation → Research → Packaging → Outline + script → Film → Edit → Repurpose → **Publish**

## Why this matters

How you publish decides whether YouTube shows your video to the right people. And every link and description points people to one place: your offer. Then, once it's live, your results tell you what to change next time.

## What you'll learn

- **Upload rules that matter.** Set upload defaults once (description template, private or unlisted by default). Never "made for kids." Always set the category. Add your own chapters and turn YouTube's automatic chapters off. Always add an end screen: subscribe + the best next video for that viewer. Pin a comment right away. Ignore tags. *(Nate Curtiss; chapters also Marcus Jones)*
- **The description.** Line 1: one link to your offer. *(Nate Curtiss)* Then a short description of the video, your channel description, and chapters. Use the same link everywhere: description, pinned comment, channel links, bio.
- **The magnifying glass.** It only burns when the light is focused on one spot. Every video, post and link points to the same next step. Each video is a tentacle that brings people back to one place: "All roads lead to Rome." *(Taki Moore)*
- **Scheduling with Blotato.** Connect your platforms once, schedule your repurposed pieces, and spread them out so the video keeps getting new traffic.
- **Why it compounds.** YouTube is a search engine, and search brings "higher quality leads" when you have an offer to send them to. *(Aprilynne Alter's course)*
- **Read your results.** "Post and forget" for the first day. *(Nate Curtiss)* Numbers "tell you where to look," not what to do. *(Jake Trinder)*
- **Diagnose in order.** Was it a click problem (title, thumbnail, topic), a watch problem (retention), or an idea problem? *(Aprilynne Alter)*
- **What to watch.** Retention at 30 seconds (aim for 60–70%), average view duration compared with your other videos, and a flat retention graph. *(Nate Curtiss)*
- **Satisfaction over retention.** The best early signal is positive comments compared with views. Jake's training had about 12% average retention and still got 800K+ views. *(Jake Trinder)*
- **Don't delete flops; repackage them.** Two of Aprilynne's videos stalled at about 1,000 views, then passed 200,000. *(Aprilynne Alter)*

## Start with your publish plan

If you ran `/publish-pack` in Chapter 8, your video folder has `finished/PUBLISH PLAN.md`. Open it first. It lists what's in each numbered folder, where each piece goes, which day to post it, and a checklist. Each reel and carousel has its captions file next to it, one section per platform. The steps below cover the upload itself and how to read your results.

## Steps

1. **Set your upload defaults once.** Use [`templates/upload-checklist.md`](templates/upload-checklist.md).
2. **Write the description.** Run [`prompts/write-description.md`](prompts/write-description.md). It follows [`templates/description-template.md`](templates/description-template.md), with your offer link on line 1.
3. **Upload.** Add chapters, the end screen, and a pinned comment. Tick off the checklist.
4. **Schedule your repurposed pieces** in Blotato (or post them by hand). Follow the day-by-day calendar in `PUBLISH PLAN.md`, and tick its checklist first.
5. **Post and forget** for the first day.
6. **Read your results.** Take screenshots of your YouTube Studio analytics and run [`prompts/read-my-results.md`](prompts/read-my-results.md). Write what you learned in `videos/<your-video>/08-publish.md`, and feed it into your next idea.

## Tips

- **Launching a new channel?** Launch with 4 videos, not 1. *(Aprilynne Alter)*
- **Track average views per video and revenue,** not subscriber count. *(Paddy Galloway, in Aprilynne Alter's course; Ed Lawrence)*
- **Delete YouTube Studio from your phone.** *(Nate Curtiss)*
- **Only A/B test** once you average 3,000+ views a video. *(Nate Curtiss)*
