# Chapter 6: Film

**Stage 5 of 8:** Ideation → Research → Packaging → Outline + script → **Film** → Edit → Repurpose → Publish

## Why this matters

The thinking's done. Now we make it. Because we did the hard part first, filming is the easy part, so this chapter is light on theory. The one thing to get right is sound: "If viewers can't hear you correctly, they'll click away." *(Aprilynne Alter)*

## What you'll learn

- **Audio > lighting > camera.** Audio quality is "the most important thing." *(Nate Curtiss)* People forgive OK video. They don't forgive bad audio. If you buy one thing, buy a good mic.
- **You don't need expensive gear.** MKBHD's first video was "Um, bought a new laptop." *(Aprilynne Alter)* Your phone or laptop camera is fine to start.
- **Lighting comes right after audio.** Free: a window during the day. *(Aprilynne Alter)* Cheap: a lamp or LED lights, then a ring light or softboxes. *(Nate Curtiss)*
- **The room.** Film somewhere quiet. Kill echo with pillows, blankets and rugs. *(Aprilynne Alter)* Camera at eye level, a little space above your head, clean background.
- **Being on camera.** Look at the lens, not yourself on the screen. Warm up: hit record and talk about anything for a minute first. Mistakes are fine; editing is your safety net. *(Aprilynne Alter)*
- **Talk to strangers, not friends.** 99.999% of viewers have never heard of you. *(Ed Lawrence)*
- **Teleprompter or bullets?** Some creators riff from bullets. *(Nate Curtiss)* I use a teleprompter with one sentence per line. Pick what makes you sound natural.
- **Match production to the promise.** Jake Trinder's 800K-view training was "one camera in the presentation." Raw was what people clicked for. *(Jake Trinder)*
- **Not on camera?** Some huge channels, like Kurzgesagt, never show a host's face. *[Coming soon: faceless videos and AI avatars, and how to make them]*

## Steps

1. **Pick your gear level.** Use [`templates/gear-levels.md`](templates/gear-levels.md). Level 1 is enough to start.
2. **Set up the room and light.** A quiet room, soft things to kill echo, and a window or a lamp for light.
3. **Prep your script for reading.** Run [`prompts/teleprompter-format.md`](prompts/teleprompter-format.md) to turn it into teleprompter lines or bullets.
4. **Record a 1-minute test.** Listen back with headphones before you film the whole thing.
5. **Record.** I use Borumi, which records your screen and camera at the same time: plug in the mic and camera, hit record. Any recorder works.
6. **Take thumbnail photos** while you're still set up.

## Check it

- [ ] You listened to a test with headphones before filming the whole thing.
- [ ] The audio is clean: no echo or background noise.
- [ ] You can see your face clearly.
- [ ] The camera is at eye level, with a clean background.
- [ ] You took your thumbnail photos.

## Tips

- **Take thumbnail photos while you're still set up.** *(Aprilynne Alter)*
- **Batch film 3–4 scripts in one setup.** *(Ed Lawrence)*
