# Chapter 4: Packaging (title and thumbnail)

**Stage 3 of 8:** Ideation → Research → **Packaging** → Script → Film → Edit → Repurpose → Publish

## Why this matters

Your video sits next to 20 others in the feed, and people pick one. *(Jake Trinder)* "Why would you spend 10% of your time on something that determines 50% of the result?" *(Aprilynne Alter)* The title and thumbnail make one promise together, and the video has to keep it. *(Jake Trinder)* We package after research, so we know what the video can actually promise.

## What you'll learn

- **Outliers are where good packaging comes from.** An outlier is a video "five or 10 times your average." *(Paddy Galloway, in Aprilynne Alter's course)*
- **One winner isn't a pattern.** Find 4+ similar outliers. *(Jake Trinder, Nate Curtiss)* Small channels that broke out have 5x more views than subscribers. *(Marcus Jones)*
- **Copy the pattern, not the video.** Know why it worked. *(Jake Trinder)*
- **Three click emotions.** Curiosity (the most important), fear, desire. *(Jake Thomas, in Aprilynne Alter's course)*
- **Title moves.** Bold statements beat questions. Contrast works ("luxury fashion is for broke people"). Borrow title patterns from nearby niches: "10 best credit cards" becomes "10 best trout lures."
- **Proven title formats.** "How to [result] so fast it feels like cheating." "If you don't understand X, you don't understand Y." *(Jake Trinder)*
- **Keep titles short:** under about 50 characters. *(Ed Lawrence)*
- **The thumbnail is "80, maybe 90% the psychology of the click."** *(Paddy Galloway, quoted by Aprilynne Alter)* It has two jobs: get picked out of the feed, then get understood at a glance. *(Jake Trinder)*
- **The 3 C's of a thumbnail.** Contents (one main subject), composition, contrast. Text: 5 words max, and never repeat the title. *(Aprilynne Alter)*
- **Title + thumbnail work together.** The title makes them interested; the thumbnail adds intrigue. *(Jon Dorman)*
- **Scroll stoppers.** A face, money or big numbers, emotion, bright color. *(Aprilynne Alter)*

## Steps

1. **Pull 4+ similar outliers** for your topic (from Chapter 2).
2. **Write lots of titles.** Aprilynne Alter's drill: 30 titles in 20 minutes, including 5 from your head, 5 from other niches, 5 from outliers, and 5 with AI.
3. **Score them.** Run [`prompts/title-scoring.md`](prompts/title-scoring.md) with your titles. Pick your top 3. *(Nate Curtiss)*
4. **Thumbnail concepts.** Run [`prompts/thumbnail-concepts.md`](prompts/thumbnail-concepts.md). Then make 3 real ones. *(Nate Curtiss)*
5. **Test it in a feed.** Put your thumbnail and title in a screenshot of a YouTube feed, next to your competitors. Then shrink it to phone size.
6. **Save** your pick in `my-video/03-packaging.md`.

## Check it

- [ ] **Clarity:** is it readable at phone size?
- [ ] **Contrast:** does it stand out next to competitors?
- [ ] **Glance:** show it to someone for 2 seconds. Can they say what it's about? *(Aprilynne Alter)*
- [ ] Do the title and thumbnail make one promise the video can keep?
- [ ] Is the thumbnail text 5 words or fewer, and different from the title?

## Tips

- **Package before you film,** and take thumbnail photos while you're set up.
- **A low click-through rate** (the share of people who saw it and clicked) means look closer, not swap automatically. *(Jake Trinder)*
- **Only A/B test** (try two versions against each other) once you average 3,000+ views a video. *(Nate Curtiss)*
